---
title: Self-standing artifacts
applicability:
- Before writing an artifact that will be used without the process that produced it
---

An artifact's reader has neither your process nor the versions before it, so whatever the artifact says about how it came to be reads as part of what it is: a rejected option reads as a constraint, as authority to act, or as a question still open, and what holds is buried among it. Unless the artifact exists to record that history, write it as what holds, in terms its reader can resolve. Do not define it against an earlier version, an option weighed or rejected, the conversation that shaped it, or an assumption only the process held.

State a boundary in the artifact only where a reader not told of it would act across it or take what lies beyond it as covered. Write it as part of what holds: the extent of what the artifact covers or permits, and, where something beyond it is still open, who decides it. That an option was considered, refused, or ruled out is process however it is marked, and naming a boundary the reader would never approach introduces the option it excludes.

Reasoning about what holds, such as why a rule is right, belongs in the artifact when its purpose includes reasoning. Reasoning about the change is process, not evidence to that reader: what the artifact said before, why it changed, and what was weighed and dropped. Put each only in a record or report whose purpose includes it, such as a change record for what changed and why or a decision record for the options weighed and each one's status, and never beside the changed value merely because it explains the change. A superseded or rejected option establishes nothing about what holds, so it appears only in such a record or report, marked there with its status.

Before delivering the artifact, read it as its reader would, holding only the artifact, and remove each statement that changes nothing they would do or take to hold about its subject. A statement about the artifact itself rather than its subject, such as its intended reader or its progress, stays only when its reader acts on it, as on a prerequisite to meet before using it or a status saying whether it is still in force; otherwise it reports on the work that produced it and belongs in the report to whoever asked for that work.
